# The Green Team — host-led UGC campaign

The second campaign, running beside `agartha-ugc/campaign/` rather than
replacing it. They do different jobs and must not be confused:

| | Permaculture films | This |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sells | Agartha, one project | The Green Team, the business |
| On screen | Objects and spaces. **No people.** | Tara, the host |
| Form | 30s voiceover film, question ending | Short talking-head + location cuts |
| Kills | Four specific farmland objections | "why go through a middleman at all" |

## The concept: *We looked so you don't have to*

The permaculture films argue for a **property**. This argues for **us** — and
the only honest argument for a channel partner is that we did the work of
elimination before the buyer arrived. Every piece is the same shape:

> **Here is a thing buyers get wrong → here is what we actually check →
> here is what we found.**

Tara is the one who went and looked. That is her whole job on screen. She is
not a customer, she has not bought anything, and she never reports owning or
earning — she reports **checking**.

### The four objections, one piece each

| # | The objection | What she shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Every listing says forest view." | What we measure on site — air, noise, distance — and what fails |
| 2 | "A broker just wants the commission." | We are paid by the developer, so the buyer's cost is unchanged — say it plainly |
| 3 | "I'll just go direct to the builder." | Direct gets you one project's version of itself; we hold three and will talk you out of one |
| 4 | "Site visits are a sales trap." | What a visit with us actually looks like, and what we tell you not to buy |

Number 2 is the uncomfortable one and therefore the one worth making. A
channel partner explaining its own economics unprompted is the single most
trust-building thing in this category.

## Rules for the host

**Wardrobe changes every shoot day.** The first build locked her into one
olive kurta across every frame, because the identity block bundled clothing in
with the face. That instantly reads as fake — nobody wears one outfit to four
locations. Identity is pinned to her **face only**; wardrobe is a per-shot
variable, and the shot list below sets it per piece.

**She is a host, not a customer.** She can say what we check, what a place is
like, and what we rejected. She cannot say she bought, owns, earns, or lives
anywhere.

**She is disclosed.** ASCI's influencer guidelines cover virtual and
AI-generated presenters, and "UGC" is the framing that makes it sharp — the
format's power is the implication a real person filmed it. Every placement
carries a visible **"Virtual host · AI-generated"** label.

**No price, rate, appreciation figure or RERA number** in any cut. Same rule
as the films. Those belong in the DM reply where there is room for context.

## The honest limitation

Our sources are **architectural renders**. Compositing a person into a render
produces a render with a person in it, and the eye catches it even when it
cannot say why — clean light, no dust, no clutter, nobody else there. Prompting
does not close that gap.

What closes it is **real photography**: the site, the approach road, the
planting, the goshala, the team. Even phone photos. One real photograph is
worth ten renders for this format, because UGC's entire claim is that someone
was actually there.

Until that exists, this campaign is capped at "good composite" and should be
run as **brand content**, not as anything implying a real person's visit.
