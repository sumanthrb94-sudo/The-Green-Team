Build blueprint

Telugu voice agent, from open source

A teardown of the Outpero Telugu calling agents, and a costed plan to build the equivalent yourself — at roughly a third of the running cost.

Researched Aug 2026 Prices INR, list For The Green Team

Part 1

What Outpero actually is

Read their homepage and their Telugu page together and the shape is unambiguous. They are an automation agency, not a voice-AI lab. The homepage sells three "systems" — Revenue Capture from ₹60,000, Ops Efficiency from ₹1,00,000, Web Capture from ₹50,000 — plus 19 pre-built solutions from ₹14,999, delivered "live in 2 weeks." That's a services business with a fixed build kit.

The tell is on their own homepage: the Ops system lists "Custom n8n/Make Logic" as a deliverable. n8n is the glue. The voice layer is bought, not built.

Their margin is the gap between ~₹4/min of vendor cost and what they bill. There is no moat here.

Why the demos sound ultra-realistic

Ranked by contribution. This ordering is the most useful thing in this document — it tells you where to spend your two weeks.

  1. 50% A cloned voice from a real Telugu speaker Stock te-IN voices sound like a railway announcement. Sixty seconds of a real Hyderabad salesperson, cloned, sounds like a person — because it is one. This is the whole game; everything below is polish.
  2. 15% Native-script text normalization ₹68.7 L must reach the TTS as అరవై ఎనిమిది లక్షల డెబ్బై వేల. Every engine mangles digits, currency and initialisms in Indic scripts, and one mangled number ends the illusion instantly.
  3. 15% Under ~800 ms to first audio Humans reply in 200–500 ms. Past a second of silence the caller says "hello?" and the spell breaks. An engineering budget, not a model choice.
  4. 10% Short turns Cap the agent at two sentences / ~35 Telugu words. A twenty-second uninterrupted monologue is the single clearest giveaway of a bot.
  5. 5% Barge-in and backchannels The caller must be able to cut the agent off mid-sentence and have it stop instantly. Sprinkle హా సర్, అవును, సరే while the LLM thinks.
  6. 5% The phone codec is helping them PSTN is 8 kHz μ-law, which strips exactly the high frequencies where TTS artifacts live. The same voice that sounds slightly synthetic in a browser sounds human on a call.

Their website demo players are almost certainly full-band studio renders. Never judge a voice on studio playback — A/B every candidate through an actual phone call before assuming they are ahead of what you can build in a week.

TTS is 55% of the bill. That is the number to attack.

Stack B — speech-to-speech

Gemini Live native audio: no STT→LLM→TTS chain, ~300 ms response, Telugu supported, ~$0.036/min ≈ ₹3.15, plus telephony ₹0.75 → ≈ ₹3.90/min. Fewest moving parts and the best latency — but you cannot use your own cloned voice, and the cloned voice is half the realism. Wrong trade for a sales agent trying to sound like a specific human; right choice for an inbound receptionist where neutral-but-fast wins. Build it in week 1 as your latency baseline; it takes an afternoon.

Break-even. Self-hosting saves ~₹2.25/min. A GPU running 24/7 costs ~₹816/day. You need ~360 call-minutes/day (6 hours of talk time) to justify it.

Build Stack A, instrument it, migrate to Stack C past ~350 call-min/day. Starting at C means three weeks on GPU autoscaling instead of on the conversation — which is where all the quality actually lives.

Turn-taking — do not skip this

  • snakers4/silero-vad — the baseline VAD everyone uses. MIT, tiny, CPU.
  • LiveKit turn detector (in livekit/agents) — semantic end-of-turn detection; knows "నేను అనుకుంటున్నాను…" isn't finished. Cuts false interrupts dramatically.
  • Smart Turn (in pipecat) — the equivalent on that side.

Endpointing is the difference between an agent that talks over people and one that doesn't. Budget real tuning time — it's the most under-rated component in every voice stack.

Part 4

The realism checklist

Ship none of these and you have a robot. Ship all of them and you're at parity.

  1. Clone a real voice. Hire a Telugu voice actor for two hours. Record 10–15 minutes clean — no room echo, no compression, 48 kHz — reading the objection blocks from the cold-call script, so you capture the sales register, not neutral narration. Get a signed release covering synthetic reproduction. This one step outranks every model choice.
  2. Normalize text before TTS. Numbers, currency, dates and initialisms into Telugu words. Praxy ships indic_numbers.py for exactly this — steal it. Never send ₹68.7L or AQI 12 raw.
  3. Cap turn length in the system prompt. "At most 2 sentences, under 35 Telugu words, never more than 3 items." Enforce it — truncate server-side when the LLM overruns.
  4. Stream TTS sentence-by-sentence. Start speaking on sentence one while the rest generates. Halves perceived latency for free.
  5. Hold sub-800 ms to first audio. Typical budget: VAD close 250 ms → STT final 150 ms → LLM first token 250 ms → TTS first chunk 150 ms. Instrument each stage separately.
  6. Barge-in that actually stops. On detected speech, kill the audio buffer immediately — not at the end of the current chunk.
  7. Filler while thinking. If the LLM hasn't returned in 400 ms, play a pre-rendered "హా సర్…". Pre-render once; never synthesize live.
  8. Warm human transfer. SIP REFER on "మనిషితో మాట్లాడాలి". Test it on day one — it's the most business-critical path in the system.
  9. Handle Tanglish input. Real callers say "sir, price entha?" Your STT must not drop the English, and your prompt must not correct the caller's register.
  10. Tune on 8 kHz. Evaluate every voice through a real PSTN call, never laptop speakers.
  11. Re-listen weekly. Pull 20 real calls, mark every moment you'd have hung up, fix the top three. This loop is what separates a demo from a product — and it's the only part nobody can sell you.
  12. Disclose the AI in the first sentence. Compliance, yes — but also, callers who feel deceived hang up harder and complain louder.

Part 5

India compliance, non-negotiable

Outbound commercial calling in India in 2026 is regulated and actively enforced — TRAI's own detection models look for synthetic-voice signatures and abnormal call patterns. Over 47,000 numbers were disconnected in Q1 2026 alone, including legitimate businesses with sloppy DLT registration. Penalties run to ₹10 lakh.

Before your first live dial:

  • Register as a telemarketer on the DLT platform — entity, sender IDs, and your call scripts.
  • Use the correct number series — 140 promotional, 1600 transactional. Promotional traffic from a normal 10-digit mobile is the fastest route to disconnection.
  • Scrub every list against DND before each campaign, not once.
  • Capture revocable consent with timestamp and source. A Meta Lead Ad submission is consent for that enquiry — log the lead ID against the call record.
  • Call only 09:00–21:00 local time.
  • Disclose the AI in the opening line and label synthetic voice per the 2026 IT Rules amendment. Never impersonate a named real person.
  • Honour opt-out instantly and permanently — wire it to a hard suppression list, not a CRM tag.
  • DPDP Act: recordings and transcripts are personal data. Define retention, restrict access, support deletion on request.

The cheapest stack in the world is worthless if the numbers get cut off in week three.

Part 6

Fourteen days

  1. Days 1–2 Voice asset Book the voice actor. Record 15 minutes. Clone into ElevenLabs and IndicF5 from the same source clip, A/B them on the 17-clip test set — through a phone call, not speakers. Pick a winner.
  2. Days 3–4 Local loop LiveKit Agents locally, browser mic, no telephony: Saarika → Gemini Flash → your voice. Get one full turn working. Spend an afternoon on Gemini Live speech-to-speech as the latency baseline to beat.
  3. Days 5–6 Conversation design Port the nine blocks and seven objection handles into the system prompt as a state machine: greet → qualify → pitch → objection → close → book/transfer. Add the turn cap and the Telugu normalizer. Give this the most time — it's where the quality is.
  4. Days 7–8 Telephony Plivo trunk → LiveKit SIP, outbound and inbound. Test warm transfer. Start DLT registration now — approval takes days and it will be your critical path.
  5. Days 9–10 Latency and endpointing Instrument the four-stage budget. Add turn detection, barge-in, pre-rendered fillers. Target sub-800 ms. This is the gap between "impressive" and "real".
  6. Days 11–12 Data loop n8n: Meta Lead Ad → Supabase → trigger call → post-call summary → back to Supabase. Build the minimal Voice Hub view: calls, duration, outcome, transcript, recording, intent score.
  7. Days 13–14 Pilot and regression set Fifty real calls to consented leads. Listen to all fifty. Build a fixed 20-case Telugu regression suite from the failures and re-run it on every prompt change, forever.

Then, and only then: if you're past ~350 call-min/day, migrate STT and TTS onto an L4 and take the bill from ₹3.25 to ₹1.00 per minute.

Bottom line

Outpero is a well-executed assembly job on bought parts, sold at agency prices. Nothing on that page requires technology you cannot self-host this month.

Your real costs: ~₹3.25/min managed, ~₹1.00/min self-hosted, plus one voice actor (₹5–15k, one-time) and about two weeks of build time. What decides whether it sounds ultra-realistic is not the framework — it's the quality of that one voice recording, whether your numbers are spelled out in Telugu, and whether you keep the agent's turns short.

Everything else is plumbing.
